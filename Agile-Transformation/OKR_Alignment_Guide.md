OKR Alignment Guide v1.0
Purpose

Objectives and Key Results (OKRs) help organizations align strategy, execution, and measurable outcomes across leadership, portfolios, Agile Release Trains (ARTs), teams, and individuals.

The purpose of OKRs is to ensure everyone is working toward the same business outcomes while maintaining flexibility in execution.

What Are OKRs?
Objective

A qualitative statement describing what we want to achieve.

Example

"Improve Digital Customer Experience"

Key Results

Quantifiable outcomes that measure success.

Example
Increase mobile app rating from 3.8 to 4.5
Reduce onboarding time from 7 days to 1 day
Increase digital adoption by 20%
Why OKRs Matter

Organizations often struggle with:

Too many priorities
Misaligned initiatives
Activity-focused execution
Lack of measurable outcomes

OKRs create alignment and focus.

OKR Planning Process
Step 1

Define Strategic Priorities

Questions:

What are our biggest business challenges?
What outcomes matter most?
Step 2

Create Enterprise OKRs

Leadership defines:

Objectives
Key Results
Success Metrics
Step 3

Cascade Alignment

Portfolio → ART → Team

Focus on alignment, not command-and-control.

Step 4

Use:

PI Planning
Sprint Planning
Backlog Prioritization

to support OKR achievement.

Step 5

Review Progress

Recommended Cadence:

Monthly Reviews
Quarterly Reviews
PI Reviews

Common OKR Anti-Patterns
Too Many OKRs

Focus is lost.

Recommended:

3–5 Objectives per level.

Activity-Based Key Results

Bad Example:

Conduct 10 meetings

Good Example:

Improve stakeholder satisfaction by 15%
Lack of Alignment

Teams pursue local goals.

Focus on enterprise outcomes.

No Review Cadence

OKRs become documents instead of management tools.
